# RFM customer segmentation · e-commerce

Splits a customer base into five groups by how recently, how often and how much they buy, and proposes a sales
action for each group.

**Result:** the 246 best customers are 26 % of the base and 66.5 % of sales. At the other end, 401 customers, 42 %
of the base, add up to 3.8 % of sales.

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
jupyter notebook 01_Analisis_RFM_Completo.ipynb
```

The data is synthetic: 946 customers and 7,741 orders over twelve months, generated by the repository itself with
`Script_Datos_Sinteticos.ipynb`.

## The problem

A sales team cannot call every customer each week, and it nearly always sorts by turnover. That order hides the
valuable customer who has stopped buying. In this data, customer 1029 bought twenty-two times and spent 20,637
dollars, and has not come back for two months. The yearly total puts them at the top and gives no warning that they
are leaving.

## The method

**RFM** stands for recency, frequency and monetary value. Three numbers are computed for each customer:
- recency: days since the last purchase;
- frequency: number of purchases;
- monetary value: total money spent.

Each number is scored from 1 to 5 by quintiles, that is, by cutting the sorted base into five equal parts. The
cut-offs come from the data itself rather than from a threshold set by eye:

| Cut-off | Recency | Frequency | Monetary |
|---|---|---|---|
| p20 | 13 days | 2 purchases | $405 |
| p40 | 28 days | 4 purchases | $1,246 |
| p60 | 59 days | 7 purchases | $3,537 |
| p80 | 121 days | 13 purchases | $8,489 |

The three scores form a code, and the code assigns the group by the first rule it meets:

```
Champions → R>=4, F>=4, M>=4
Leales    → R>=3, F>=3, M>=3   (loyal)
Nuevos    → R>=4, F<=2         (new)
En riesgo → R<=2, F>=3, M>=3   (at risk)
Otros     → the rest           (others)
```

## The five groups

| Segment | Customers | % | Average spend | Days since last purchase | Purchases |
|---------|-----------|---|---------------|--------------------------|-----------|
| Champions | 246 | 26 % | $15,447 | 12 | 16.8 |
| Loyal | 197 | 21 % | $6,212 | 33 | 9.3 |
| At risk | 102 | 11 % | $4,587 | 96 | 8.1 |
| New | 59 | 6 % | $528 | 13 | 2.5 |
| Others | 342 | 36 % | $550 | 144 | 2.3 |

## Proposed actions

- Champions: a preferred-customer programme, with early access and an account manager.
- At risk: a 30-day win-back campaign with a discount and ads shown to people who already visited the shop.
- New: support during the first 90 days with automated emails and a rising discount.

## How it was checked

The notebook runs nine checks on its own result before drawing anything; for instance, that Champions have on
average fewer than thirty days since their last purchase. The three correlations between each score and its source
number go the expected way: recency −0.83, frequency 0.87 and monetary 0.76.

![Correlation](03_correlacion_heatmap.png)

The figures on this page were recomputed with a separate program that reads the orders without pandas, and they
match. Both notebooks run from start to finish in a fresh environment installed only from `requirements.txt`, and
the generator reproduces the data file exactly.

## Limits

- The data is invented. It teaches the method without publishing real customers.
- RFM only looks at sales. It knows nothing of each product's margin, the cost of serving each customer or why
  someone left.
- The quintile cut-off moves when the base changes, and two almost identical customers can fall on different sides:
  with 60 days without buying, customer 1029 is At risk, while 1120, with 59 days and the same purchases, is Loyal.
